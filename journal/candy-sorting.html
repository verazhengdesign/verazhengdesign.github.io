Candy Sorting

Sep 3, 2024

Candy sorting is an activity where participants organize a variety of candies into categories based on different criteria. These criteria may include color, shape, size, flavor, brand, or personal preferences.

Sorting tasks are often highlighted to engage participants in developing critical thinking and organizational skills. I believe this is why, as the first class in our Information Architecture course, candy sorting is an interesting introduction to help us understand the basics of IA.

During the first class for the information architecture course, I teamed up with two classmates to form a small group. Following the professor’s instructions, we sorted candies based on different criteria. We categorized them by flavor, type, packaging shape, and color.

For each criterion, we created a corresponding visual representation.

One of my group members sorted the candies by flavor, and then by colour.

This is my work. I first sorted the candy by color, and then sorted them by packaging shape.

Another group member sorted candies by packaging shape and then by flavor and packaging methods.

During the group discussions, other members expressed their appreciation for our packaging-based sorting method, calling it creative and innovative.

We also discovered that other groups had approached the task differently, using criteria like texture, ingredient, and soft or hard. This experience underscored the value of exploring multiple perspectives and revealed how multi-layered classification systems can be applied in information architecture.

As someone who had never encountered information architecture before, this was my first introduction to the subject through both activities and coursework. The experience was enjoyable and memorable — I especially liked interacting with new classmates and sharing delicious candies while learning together.
