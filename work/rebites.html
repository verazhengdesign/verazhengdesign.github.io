Context

The problem

[Draft] Kids learn better when sustainability is playful and concrete, but most tools are text-heavy and adult-facing. Teachers, on the other hand, need something quick to run in a classroom.

  • Low awareness of where food waste actually happens.
  • Existing content wasn’t built for a young audience.
  • Teachers lacked an easy, repeatable way to teach it.
Research

What I learned

[Draft] Through market validation and secondary research, I found that people are generally aware of food waste and willing to act, but struggle with convenience and motivation.

  • Awareness isn’t the gap — habits are.
  • Make it social and rewarding, not another chore.
  • Meet kids where they are — games, points, and real-world trips.
Design

The approach

[Draft] I designed a dual interface: a playful student space and a lightweight teacher dashboard. Learning happens through short, repeatable activities.

  • Mini-game “Flip the Table” — creative food reuse (e.g. pineapple peel → fabric).
  • Checklists and fun facts that build daily habits.
  • A points system redeemable at local markets.
  • Virtual + real farm trips for hands-on learning.
