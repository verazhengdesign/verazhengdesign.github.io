Context

The problem

[Draft] Students often struggle with budgeting, saving, and basic financial concepts — which can lead to poor decisions and long-term consequences. Existing tools are built for adults and feel like homework.

  • No clear picture of where money goes each month.
  • Financial jargon and complex features get in the way.
  • Habits break the moment a month goes wrong.
Research

What I learned

[Draft] My team and I ran semi-structured interviews with 8 financially independent students aged 21–25, exploring daily spending, awareness of budgeting issues, and goals.

  • Simplicity beats features. Students want clarity, not a finance toolkit.
  • Guidance over data. Seeing numbers isn’t enough — people need a next step.
  • Habits need rewards to survive a bad month.

We mapped pain points into a persona and journey map, then used a feasibility-priority matrix to decide which features to build first.

Design

The approach

[Draft] I turned the research into four high-level pillars and designed around them, moving from wireframes to a usability test, iteration, and final mock-ups.

  • Clarify spending without overwhelming.
  • Guide a small next step, every day.
  • Reward consistency, and forgive the gaps.
  • Keep the language plain.
