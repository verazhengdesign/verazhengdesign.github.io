Overview

What This Project Is

LemonWise is a mobile app that helps young adults — especially university students — improve their financial literacy and build better money-management habits.

Designed over a semester with a team of four, from interviews through to a tested, high-fidelity prototype.

Context

The Problem

Many students — and those just entering the workforce — struggle with budgeting, saving, and understanding basic financial concepts, which can lead to poor financial decisions and long-term consequences.

I just graduated and I’m making my own money for the first time. What do I do now?!

LemonWise responds with a simple, intuitive tool that prioritises ease of use, clear guidance, and habit-building over complex features and technical jargon.

Research

What I Learned

My team and I conducted semi-structured interviews with 8 participants aged 21–25, all financially independent students, to understand the behaviours and struggles behind student money management.

MethodSemi-structured user interviews
Participants8 financially independent students, ages 21–25
FocusDaily spending habits, awareness of budgeting issues, personal financial goals and literacy
OutputPain points, a persona, a journey map, and a prioritization matrix

The findings point to a clear need for a simple, intuitive financial tool tailored to students — one that prioritises ease of use, clear guidance, and habit-building support over complex features.

  • Simplicity beats features. Students want clarity, not a finance toolkit.
  • Guidance over data. Seeing numbers isn’t enough — people need a clear next step.
  • Habits need rewards to survive a bad month.

User pain points

Persona & journey map

We translated the interviews into a persona and an end-to-end journey map, then used a feasibility–priority matrix to decide which features to build first.

Prioritization matrix

Design

The Approach

I turned the research into four high-level pillars — the goals every design decision had to serve — then moved from wireframes to a second usability test, iteration, and final mock-ups.

  • Spending Analytics — a simple daily view of where money goes.
  • Investment Integration — accessible, jargon-free investing.
  • Goal-Setting — organization and goal setting made simple.
  • Learning Hub — short, approachable financial literacy.

Outcome

What Changed

We ran a second usability test on the prototype, then iterated on the flows that caused friction — clarifying navigation, improving data visualization, and making the language plainer.
