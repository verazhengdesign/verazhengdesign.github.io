Overview

Project Overview

This project focused on improving Elections Ontario’s internship program by designing a structured onboarding and feedback experience through service blueprinting, frontstage and backstage process mapping, and physical prototyping to clarify intern responsibilities and strengthen ongoing support.

As the researcher and service designer on the team, I led the research and made major contributions to the design work. I conducted both 30-minute interviews, owned the data analysis, and created all of the as-is journey maps. I also built the physical and digital Responsibilities Board prototypes, and was responsible for recording, editing, and voicing the Prototype Walkthrough video.

Problem

The Problem

Elections Ontario lacks a talent pipeline to identify, train and nurture to become the next generation of election professionals.

Initial Research

Research

User Interviews

To ground the redesign in students’ lived experience, we conducted two 30-minute in-person interviews with students who had previously completed internships.

One participant had interned at the University of Toronto Innovation Hub; the other had interned at RBC Wealth Management. Their different contexts gave us a useful mix of perspectives on how internship programs support — or fail to support — students.

Research Synthesis

Through analysis of the interview transcripts, we conducted a qualitative analysis across six themes: Touchpoints, Actions, Pain Points, Needs, Ideas, Outcomes, and summarized five journey steps.

We tagged each synthesized sticky note and grouped them again.

Design

Design Strategy

To support future connections and long-term talent development, we aim to make the internship program more visible, developable, and sustainable, which also strengthens Elections Ontario’s ability to build a reliable and continuous talent pipeline for future workforce needs.

Make intern work, progress, and impact visible across Elections Ontario

Visible Work

Support skills growth, interns enable continuous improvement

Developable

Extend the value of the internship beyond a single term, provide continuous influence

Sustainable

Initial Sketch

After brainstorming and group discussion, our core idea was to create a physical Responsibilities Board. This board lives in the office and gives every intern their own row to track tasks, post questions, and see feedback. It makes day-to-day work visible and shared.

To improve the internship experience, we believed that person-to-person interaction mattered most. At the same time, moving away from a screen-filled world to a physical board that people can interact with felt like a strong direction.

Service Blueprint

Based on our idea, we created a service blueprint showing how interns use the physical board.

This blueprint shows the entire internship journey across three stages: before onboarding, during the internship, and offboarding. It maps both the intern’s frontstage experience and the backstage support from Elections Ontario staff.

Outcome

Outcome

This project spanned four months. I had the privilege of working with my wonderful mentor, my teammates, and the Elections Ontario stakeholders.

Building the physical prototype was more challenging than we expected, but it was also genuinely fun. Despite the many twists and turns, I am proud of what we created and grateful for the chance to present the final product to our stakeholders.

We received very positive feedback. The stakeholder was especially drawn to the physical Responsibilities Board. She felt that in a world dominated by digital screens, creating an opportunity for people to stand up, walk around, and communicate face-to-face was a meaningful and valuable strategy.
