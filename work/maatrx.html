What this project is

Context

The problem

[Draft] Users needed to answer one question fast: which shortages affect me, right now? The old dashboard surfaced everything at once, so the signal was buried in the detail.

What made it hard

  • Dense tables with no clear priority or hierarchy.
  • Critical status was hard to distinguish at a glance.
  • Too many steps to move from “spotting an issue” to “acting on it”.
Research

What I learned

[Draft] I ran [X] interviews and a short moderated usability test to understand how people scan the dashboard and where they hesitated.

Key insights

  • Scan, don’t read. People look for status and severity first, then drill in.
  • Priority beats completeness. A smaller set of the right signals is more useful than everything.
  • Context matters. Users need to know whether a shortage affects their region or stock.
Design

The approach

[Draft] I restructured the dashboard around a scan-first hierarchy: status at the top, filters that match how people think, and detail on demand. I sketched and tested a few layouts before refining one.

Iterations

  • Reordered the page so severity and status read first.
  • Introduced a clearer visual language for shortage levels.
  • Moved secondary detail into expandable rows.
