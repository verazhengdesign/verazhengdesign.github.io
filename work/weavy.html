Overview

What This Project Is

Weavy is a friend-matching app designed to foster authentic, low-pressure social connections — in a world increasingly shaped by AI-driven interaction.

Made in 24 hours at the U of T Designathon 2026, with Lucy Lu and Shirley Zhao.

I joined this project as a UX researcher and designer, leading the research process from writing the discussion guide to interviewing 6 users and analyzing their responses. I created personas and identified key user behaviors and design recommendations.

On the design side, I played a key role in shaping the product logic, designed low- and high-fidelity screens for both receivers and inviters, and built the interactive prototype.

Problem

The Challenge

Gen-AI companion apps are exploding, with a 700% increase between 2022 and mid-2025. But as AI becomes more integrated into our lives, we asked: How can we create a future we actually want to live in, both online and offline?

Rather than replacing human connection, we wanted to explore how AI could help bring people together.

The Opportunity

Inspired by the Invisible String Theory, we envisioned a solution that uses an unseen thread to connect people — online and offline.

Our goal shifted from “building an AI tool” to “designing a bridge.”

Research

Discovery Phase

As the design researcher, I designed and ran the primary research: an unmoderated, open-ended survey so participants could answer honestly, without real-time pressure or bias.

Research themes

From our 10 open-ended questions, and 6 participants' answer, I synthesised the findings into three core themes.

01

Building community

How people currently build and maintain their social circles, and what challenges they face when trying to meet new friends.

Questions
  • How do you currently feel about your social life? Would you like to meet more local friends? Why or why not?
  • Through which channels do you usually make new friends? (e.g., school, work, social media, events, hobby groups)
  • When you want to join an activity but don’t have anyone to go with, what do you usually do?
02

Defining friendships

How people define meaningful friendships, including the qualities, values, and types of interactions they look for in new connections.

Questions
  • What qualities do you value most when forming new friendships?
  • In what kinds of situations or activities do you feel most comfortable meeting new people?
  • What motivates you to actively seek new friendships?
03

User expectations

What users expect from a friend-matching platform and what concerns might prevent them from using it.

Questions
  • If there were an app that matched you with people who share similar interests, what would you expect from it?
  • What concerns would you have before using it?
  • What would frustrate you most if the app did not work well?
  • What types of activities would you most like to use the app for? (e.g., grab coffee, sports, study sessions, concerts, trying new restaurants)

Meet Lucia — Our Persona

To better understand the problem space, we also created a persona based on the research results.

4 Defining Behaviours

01

Reliance on Existing Social Circles

  • Most participants primarily make friends through school, work, or mutual friends.

“When I was moving to Toronto, I felt pretty lonely. I didn’t know anyone really, it was only my roommate that I became friends with overtime.”

5/6 participants struggle to build friendships after graduation or moving.

02

Preference for Low-Pressure Social Interactions

  • Large gatherings can feel overwhelming; small groups feel more comfortable.

“When it’s usually a very small group like 1 or 2 people max. I am very introverted and shy when it’s a bigger group.”

3/6 participants identify as introverted. People want activity-based socializing, not pure networking.

03

Dependent on Friends’ Availability

  • Prefer to attend activities with friends.

“If I don’t have anyone to go with, I just go alone.”

5/6 participants will still go alone; 1/6 participant doesn’t go.

04

Strong Need for Shared Interests

  • Want to explore the city, try new hobbies, attend events.

“Wanting to meet people with similar interests to do activities together with.”

5/6 participants want partners for activities, sports, concerts, and classes.

Design Recommendations

Instead of just listing problems, we mapped participants’ major anxieties into actionable UX recommendations.

Safety and Trust

Strong safety features such as identity verification, reporting systems, and moderated profiles are essential.

4/6 participants mentioned safety concerns

Uncertainty About “Right Match”

AI-assisted matching based on shared interests, values, and activity preferences to increase match quality.

5/6 participants emphasized the need for personality fit

Fear of Time Waste

Features that encourage accountability, such as reminders, reputation signals, or reporting for repeated ghosting.

3/6 participants worried to meet wrong people

Avoid “Dating-App-Like” Experiences

Allow users to specify gender preferences to maintain a friendship-focused and comfortable community.

2/6 participants explicitly mentioned dating-app concerns
Design

Concept & Mascot

We chose a mobile app format for real-time, on-the-go coordination, and anchored our visual strategy around a friendly mascot named Weavy.

Design Process

Outcome

What Changed

We built Weavy in a 24-hour hackathon — no sleep, starting from zero. We ran the research from scratch, then designed 28 low-fidelity and 28 high-fidelity screens: the most of any team in the hackathon.

I’m especially grateful for my teammate and the way we worked together. Our communication and collaboration were close to effortless — a real collision of ideas — and I loved taking the concept from zero to one with our own hands.

Since then we’ve had several chances to present the project publicly to experienced designers and researchers, and the response has been generous.
