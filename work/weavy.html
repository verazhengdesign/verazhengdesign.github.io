Context

The problem

[Draft] Gen-AI companion apps are exploding — but they can monetise loneliness rather than relieve it. We asked: how might we design social experiences that prioritise real connection instead of replacing it?

  • AI companion apps surged ~700% between 2022 and mid-2025.
  • Young adults want connection but feel pressured by it.
  • Existing social apps reward performance, not authenticity.
Research

What I learned

[Draft] I designed an unmoderated, open-ended survey to understand how young adults navigate isolation and form community. From 10 open-ended questions I synthesised three core themes and four defining behaviours.

  • Low-pressure beats high-effort. People want connection that doesn’t feel like a performance.
  • Shared context matters more than endless matching.
  • Small, real moments beat big social gestures.
Design

The approach

[Draft] Inspired by the “invisible string theory”, we designed an unseen thread that connects people. We anchored the visuals around a friendly mascot, Weavy, and moved from sketches to a design system and a high-fidelity walkthrough.

  • Rapid low-fidelity sketching to map the core flow.
  • Mid-fidelity refinement and a small design system.
  • High-fidelity walkthrough focused on one key interaction.
